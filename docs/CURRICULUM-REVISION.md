# Short-lesson curriculum revision — 24 October 2026

## Audience and pacing

The target is a middle-school foundation for learners aged 11 and 13, with mechanisms that high-school courses can develop further. The content is not reduced to an eight-year-old level. A younger learner can join a read-aloud, inspect a picture, trace a route with a finger, or offer a prediction; these are participation options, not a different academic standard.

The design target is **about 10 minutes per lesson**, including questions and feedback. This is a planning estimate, not a measured completion time or a timer. Try roughly three minutes for reading and a figure, two for the explain-aloud prompt, and five for the five questions and feedback. Some learners will need longer. Optional structure-guide material is collapsed outside that main path.

There is no 15–25-minute lesson target and no 450–700-word minimum. Current lesson prose is approximately 151–283 words, averaging 191, excluding headings and picture captions. Visible objectives, captions, question choices, and feedback add reading beyond that prose count. Aim around 160–280 prose words for future core lessons, with flexibility for clarity; split a crowded lesson rather than pad or compress it to a rigid quota. Introductions and reviews may sit near the lower end.

Validate the estimate with the actual learners: note ordinary reading time, time on the explanation prompt, and time on five questions. If a core lesson regularly runs long, split its ideas or remove optional detail rather than lower the conceptual level. The eight-year-old’s completion time is not the pacing standard.

## Structure implemented

The seven-unit order remains. Each unit now has an introduction and a review. Seven focused lessons were added to separate crowded ideas: blood’s jobs; breathing mechanics; digestive helpers; water balance; reflexes; vision; hearing and balance.

At the target, this is about seven hours across many short sessions, excluding optional exploration, retries, and later spaced review.

The introductions pose a unit question and activate familiar observations. Reviews retrieve earlier ideas and ask learners to trace routes, compare mechanisms, or predict a broken connection. The last review adds a short whole-body explanation. Reviews deliberately reuse selected core questions for retrieval; the total of 210 authored quiz slots is not 210 distinct questions.

## Assessment and science changes

All 42 lesson quizzes have five text-based concept questions, explanatory feedback, and the existing 80% pass mark. Scientific terms remain in the explanations with definitions, but correct answers do not require spelling terms or recalling a long list of names. Distractors focus on nearby misconceptions. Answer order is shuffled by the existing quiz engine. Oral prompts add explanation and transfer beyond multiple choice.

The revision distinguishes tension from shortening, automatic from conscious muscle control, arteries by direction rather than oxygen level, breathing rate from depth, digestion from absorption and delivery, kidney filtration from selective return, tissue fluid from lymph, sensing from responding, and early defenses from targeted immune memory. Hormone effects depend on matching receptors and can have different time scales. Activity need not imply low arterial oxygen. Feedback has limits.

Reproductive organs and systems remain outside scope. The old endocrine overview containing reproductive organs was removed.

## Images and small presentation changes

The first-pass SVG placeholders were removed. Twenty-two official Servier PNGs were installed, individually inspected, and credited with source pages and CC BY 4.0. Five existing sourced figures were retained for their useful teaching roles. The structure catalog now serves as an optional illustrated reference; image-name drills are disabled because labelled reference art is not a fair identification test. Remaining diagram needs are recorded in IMAGE-BACKLOG.md.

Only small presentation/support changes accompany the content: responsive lesson images, contained catalog art without upscaling, visible image notes, a credits route, optional collapsed catalog reading, and validation of Markdown-body image references. There is no application redesign.

## Review status and existing progress

This is an editorial and source-based revision, not a specialist medical certification or a learner timing study. Image review means inspected for the stated use, not that every detail has received independent expert approval. Frontmatter makes that distinction explicit.

Existing lesson IDs and browser progress storage were preserved. Since quiz wording changed while question keys still use lesson ID plus index, saved question statistics from the first pass may correspond to different questions now. Existing completions also do not automatically imply completion of the new introductions or reviews. Before putting this revision into use with existing learner records, decide whether to export and reset through the Progress page. No existing browser progress was reset during this work.

The independent first-pass audit is retained under docs/audit/independent-2026-10-24. Its findings describe the old snapshot; this document describes the implemented changes.
