# Lesson-by-lesson review and revision briefs

Reviewed commit: `8d533afcba192bae684e1ea8ca12e0d55de528c6`. Main-prose counts exclude frontmatter, headings, image alternative text, and single-line captions; include lists and tables. Counts are diagnostic, not minimum-length rules.

The current seven-unit sequence is defensible. The recommendations below develop its existing strengths and recover the most useful elements of the earlier nine-unit outline. A diagram and a short explanation task should do instructional work in every lesson.

## Unit 1: From Cells to Systems — 714 words

### organization-01-cells-and-tissues — 209 words

Keep the four tissue categories and cell-shape/function examples. The brick analogy is a usable opening, but its limits matter: living cells act, exchange materials, and communicate. Replace the five colored circles with an actual nested example—stomach cell → lining tissue → stomach → digestive system → person. Use different tissue types in a stomach wall rather than four largely arbitrary symbols.

Add a second case, such as cardiac muscle tissue in a heart, and ask students to order the levels without the brick analogy. Assess one structure/function inference rather than a repeated definition. A full histology-identification lesson is unnecessary.

### organization-02-organs-and-systems — 266 words

Keep the multi-tissue stomach example and system overview. Change “does one main job” to allow multiple functions, using the pancreas as a later example. Label the grouped systems as teaching groups: “skeletal and muscular” are cooperating systems rather than one system. Include lymphatic fluid return, not just defense.

Replace the scope objective and quiz item with a location or function objective. Add a front/back body map here, or insert a dedicated body-map lesson. Teach subject-right/left, head/chest/abdomen/pelvis, overlap, and cutaway. Distinguish the organ stomach from the whole abdomen.

### organization-03-working-together — 239 words

Keep walking as the shared example, with an observation alternative for students who do not perform the action. Replace the unlabeled chain “Muscles → Blood → Lungs → Food” with arrows that say what is carried or communicated. Otherwise a dependency map can be mistaken for a physical transport route.

Ask students to trace one oxygen supply route and one nerve signal; the arrows should mean different things. Replace the question about which later unit covers a topic with a prediction about what a muscle loses if a supply or signal is interrupted.

## Unit 2: Support and Movement — 591 words

### support-01-bones-and-joints — 245 words

Retain support/protection/minerals/marrow, skull-ribs-spine recognition, and joint comparisons. Keep the sourced skeleton but identify the few labels students need. Move the densely labeled bone-growth plate to optional enrichment unless growth plates become an actual lesson objective.

Replace arbitrary joint blocks with a real hinge/ball-and-socket comparison or an explicitly labeled mechanical model. Add a small bone cutaway showing living tissue and blood supply. A useful check asks why bone needs blood even while protecting the brain or supporting the body.

### support-02-muscles-and-movement — 151 words

This is the shortest lesson and needs explanation before more vocabulary. Correct the claim that skeletal muscle waits for a decision; posture, reflexes, and automatic breathing are exceptions students already encounter. Teach force generation rather than equating every contraction with shortening.

The original “three kinds of muscle” figure draws the same curve three times despite describing stripes, branches, and sheets. Replace it with actual representative structures or use the existing sourced micrographs with explicit guidance about what to notice. Lifting versus holding a book is a concrete comparison; advanced contraction terminology can wait.

### support-03-skeleton-and-muscles-together — 195 words

Keep the tendon/ligament distinction and opposing muscles. The main image currently lacks a recognizable elbow, two joined bones, and meaningful tendon attachments. Replace it with matched bent/straight elbow views so students can actually trace a pull across a joint. SMART elbow anatomy is a possible base, not a finished contraction comparison.

A model can use a hinge and strings, with a sentence about what it omits. Replace “muscles without bones are a pull with nothing firm to pull on” with a statement limited to skeletal movement; smooth muscle already provides a counterexample. Ask which structure pulls, pivots, and resists motion in a new diagram.

## Unit 3: Circulation and Breathing — 614 words

### circulation-01-the-heart-and-blood — 199 words

Retain the right-heart/lung/left-heart/body route and the useful sourced flow diagram. Replace the Valentine-shaped catalog heart. Add valves and trace all four chambers in the actual teaching picture; otherwise the lesson names atria and ventricles without helping a student find them.

Blood components and heart anatomy can coexist if developed carefully, but the current text gives each only a few sentences. Show a component inset and distinguish platelets as fragments. A better question asks how a blocked lung connection affects the continuous circuit, with plausible alternative routes.

### circulation-02-blood-vessels-and-exchange — 202 words

Keep the correct away/exchange/return definitions. Add the pulmonary artery/vein contrast so students cannot substitute “artery means oxygen-rich.” Red/blue is a diagram convention, not actual blue human blood.

Replace three disconnected colored strokes with a branching artery–capillary network–vein diagram and a close-up showing exchange with tissue fluid. Trace the red cell staying in the vessel while oxygen crosses out. Replace distractors such as skull sutures and tendons with plausible vessel-function confusions.

### circulation-03-lungs-and-breathing — 213 words

Correct the reversed oxygen arrow first; keep the sourced alveoli diagram after fixing its oversized display. Distinguish moving air, exchanging gases, and using oxygen in cells. Explain chest-volume/pressure change in ordinary words and show matched inhalation/exhalation views. The diaphragm’s catalog drawing is not a reliable dome/position model.

The content is crowded despite its brevity. Splitting into airway/foodway comparison, breathing mechanics, and gas exchange would add two lessons. If retaining one lesson, cut incidental labels and add a genuine trace task: air stops at the alveolus, while oxygen crosses into blood. Include esophagus/trachea contrast.

## Unit 4: Fuel and Waste — 628 words

### fuel-01-digestion-from-mouth-to-stomach — 219 words

Keep mechanical/chemical digestion and peristalsis. Change the opening writer instruction “Do not begin with a list of organs” into a learner-facing observation. Replace the awkward stand-on-your-head sentence with a direct explanation that muscular waves move food, rather than gravity alone.

Add a small food-versus-air route comparison and distinguish stomach from abdomen. Use the sourced digestive map selectively; ask a student to locate this lesson’s three stops instead of expecting them to interpret all its labels. A transfer question could compare chewing with enzyme action on the same bite.

### fuel-02-small-intestine-and-absorption — 245 words

Keep the valuable explanation that bile disperses droplets instead of chemically cutting fat molecules. This lesson compresses four recognition targets, bile, bicarbonate, enzymes, villi, blood/lymph transport, insulin, and glucagon into one short page. Split accessory-organ contributions from absorption if a pilot confirms overload.

The main original picture lacks the pancreas, villi, and a crossing into blood/lymph. Replace it with a labeled secretion route and a villus close-up. Make digestion versus absorption explicit. Explain that both intestines absorb water. Trace a sugar through blood and mention that most dietary fat initially uses lymph, without requiring lipid chemistry. Finish the digestive route with rectum/anus matter-of-factly.

### fuel-03-kidneys-and-urine — 164 words

Keep the correct urine route and useful NCI urinary overview. Replace the Latin-labeled historical nephron with an English schematic that distinguishes blood flow, filtered fluid, reabsorption, and final urine. The keep/release lists alone do not show how useful substances can enter filtered fluid and return.

Remove the reproductive-scope quiz and repeated adult/shared-pathway statements; they are unnecessary and are not a good anatomy objective. Add a worked water-balance scenario and contrast urine with stool and exhaled carbon dioxide. A separate water-balance lesson would be justified. Clarify that most cells exchange with tissue fluid, rather than sitting directly in blood.

## Unit 5: Control and Coordination — 615 words

### control-01-brain-and-nerves — 194 words

Keep central/peripheral organization and the three broad brain-region anchors. Replace the stylized torso brain and add an actual brain/spinal-cord/peripheral-nerve map. A simple complete reflex pathway would connect sensory input, spinal processing, motor output, and a muscle; the current synapse arc is not that pathway.

Teach the difference between a spinal response and conscious awareness without suggesting that information never reaches the brain. Treat most cell-to-cell transmission as chemical rather than making an unnecessary absolute about every synapse. Eyes and ears are mentioned but never developed; add dedicated lessons if recognition and understanding of major sense organs remain in the intended scope.

### control-02-hormones-and-glands — 177 words

Replace the reproductive-inclusive overview and separate the mislabeled thyroid/pituitary catalog item. Keep selected pituitary, thyroid, adrenal, and pancreatic examples. Add hormone-target receptors, qualify “slower,” and explain the hypothalamus/pituitary relationship briefly.

Use the same pancreas picture to show two different routes: digestive secretions through a duct to intestine and hormones into blood. That is a stronger cross-system comparison than another isolated gland silhouette. Avoid teaching “master gland” as if the pituitary independently directs all hormone activity.

### control-03-homeostasis — 244 words

Keep “workable range, not a frozen number.” Replace the linear four-box figure with a closed feedback example: the response changes the variable that the sensor monitors. For blood glucose, the pancreas can both sense and respond; a brain command is not required for every loop.

Develop one example with a simple invented trend or diagram and use the second as transfer. Include the liver in the glucose example and identify the direction of correction. Ask why “negative” describes opposition to the change, rather than harm. Treat the fast/slow distinction as a tendency, not the definition of homeostasis.

## Unit 6: Protection and Defense — 619 words

### protection-01-skin-and-its-jobs — 182 words

Keep barrier, water retention, sensation, and temperature control. The sourced skin comparison is sophisticated; create a simpler teaching version showing epidermis, dermis, a sensory ending, a vessel, and a sweat gland, with deeper tissue explicitly labeled beneath. Keep the full figure as optional detail.

The prose says sweat cools; make evaporation the mechanism there as well as in the quiz. A skin–blood–nerve example during warmth ties the functions together. Vitamin D can remain a brief function example without implying that seeking unprotected sun exposure is an activity or recommendation.

### protection-02-immune-cells-and-lymph — 217 words

Keep the spleen/blood versus node/lymph comparison and the thymus mention. Correct tissue fluid versus lymph. Show capillary → tissue fluid → lymph vessel → return to blood and add where nodes fit. The original immune-team graphic gives four circles no useful relationships; convert it to a legend or replace it with actual cooperating structures.

The CRUK node figure can remain with a guided question about its entry/exit arrows; do not require B/T-cell label memorization. Explain antibodies as target-binding proteins that can neutralize or help mark targets. Avoid making all foreign material or all microbes sound harmful.

### protection-03-barriers-and-responses — 220 words

Distinguish barriers/broad innate response from targeted adaptive response/memory. The current second-line category lumps them together. Explain that responses overlap rather than acting as a perfectly separated sequence. Use “cilia” with an explanation such as tiny moving cell projections, rather than treating them as literal hairs.

Keep the scrape example but show clotting, local immune response, and skin repair doing different jobs. Replace the repeated out-of-scope paragraph with an explanation prompt. A useful prediction compares an intact barrier with a break in it without requiring a classroom wound or germ experiment.

### together-01-exercise-and-energy — 198 words

Keep the activity scenario and stored fuel from earlier meals. Distinguish increased tissue demand from inevitable low blood oxygen. Separate breathing rate from depth in the quiz. Replace repeated nonsense alternatives with believable errors about oxygen delivery, gas exchange, and nerve control.

Add a supplied activity/recovery table and ask for a claim supported by two observations. Students should distinguish observing a higher pulse from directly measuring oxygen delivery. Participation in exercise should not be required. Convert the existing exercise picture into a table or annotated map if it simply repeats the prose.

### together-02-eating-and-recovery — 224 words

Keep the transfer among digestion, transport, blood glucose, water balance, and repair. Replace the picture’s strict chain “Digest → Absorb → Blood carries → Insulin helps → Kidneys clean” with branches showing concurrent jobs; it implies that the whole meal proceeds into kidney cleanup after insulin.

Choose one main explanation task and use the cut as a short second case, rather than racing through both. Ask students to separate the route of food, the route of an absorbed nutrient, and the route of urine. Replace the vague sleepy/full quiz item with a specific taught signal or prediction.

### together-03-final-connections — 280 words

Keep the return to the levels of organization and the question about what a part connects to. Replace “low oxygen during work” and the reproduction-policy quiz. The current final quiz mostly repeats definitions and memorable statements.

Use this as a capstone: provide an unfamiliar but ordinary scenario and evidence, ask for three cooperating systems, trace materials and signals separately, and name one limitation. Score accuracy, causal pathway, cooperation, evidence, and model limits. This turns “how would I check?” from a closing slogan into work the student actually does.

## Five draft replacement questions

These illustrate plausible contrasts. They are recommendations, not edits to current quiz files.

1. **A vessel carries oxygen-poor blood from the heart to the lungs. Is it an artery because it carries blood away from the heart, a vein because it carries oxygen-poor blood, or a capillary because it exchanges gases?** Answer: artery. Direction defines artery/vein; exchange happens later in pulmonary capillaries.
2. **Which statement best describes glucose in normal kidney function: it can enter filtered fluid and then be returned to blood; any glucose entering filtered fluid must leave in urine; or useful substances cannot enter filtered fluid at all?** Answer: it can enter and be returned. Reabsorption is distinct from initial filtration.
3. **A hormone reaches two tissues but only one responds. Which explanation fits: only one has matching receptors; hormones act only in tissue touching the gland; or a hormone stops traveling after reaching its first tissue?** Answer: matching receptors. Transport and response are different ideas.
4. **A person takes the same number of breaths each minute, but each breath moves more air. Did breathing depth increase, breathing frequency increase, or both necessarily increase?** Answer: depth. The two measurements need not change together.
5. **Which event is absorption: enzymes splitting a large food molecule, a small nutrient crossing the intestinal lining into transport fluid, or intestinal muscles pushing food onward?** Answer: the crossing. The other actions are chemical digestion and propulsion.

## Draft capstone task

Use an **invented instructional dataset**, not clinical reference values:

| Stage | Pulse, beats/min | Breathing, breaths/min | Observation |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Resting | 80 | 16 | Comfortable |
| After activity | 120 | 28 | Warmer and sweating |
| During recovery | 90 | 19 | Cooling; less out of breath |

**Prompt:** Explain how at least three systems cooperate during activity and recovery. Name the relevant organs, use two observations as evidence, trace one material route and one signal route, and state one thing the table alone cannot establish.

**Expected:** A defensible connection among muscles, respiratory/circulatory support, nervous control, and/or skin cooling. Higher pulse and breathing are observations consistent with increased demand; they are not direct oxygen-delivery measurements or a health diagnosis. Score five criteria—anatomy, pathway, cooperation, evidence, model limits—0–2 each. Use 8/10 as an initial target and require correction of major pathway errors. This task can be a paper/self-check activity without changing the app.
