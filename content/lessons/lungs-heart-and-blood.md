## Two systems working together

Your body needs a steady supply of oxygen and a way to remove carbon dioxide. The respiratory and circulatory systems work together to do both jobs. The respiratory system brings oxygen into your body and sends carbon dioxide out. The circulatory system carries those gases, along with other materials, between the lungs and your body's cells.

## Air travels into your lungs {image-right 1-1 title-in-image}

The main parts of the respiratory system are the lungs and the airways that lead to them. When you breathe in through your nose or mouth, air travels down your windpipe. The windpipe splits into branching airways that carry air into both lungs.

Inside the lungs, the smallest airways lead to millions of tiny air sacs. When you breathe out, air travels back through the airways and out through your nose or mouth.

![Arrows trace inhaled air from the nose or mouth down the windpipe into branching airways in both lungs. An inset enlarges a cluster of air sacs.](/images/lungs-heart-and-blood/air-route.webp "Air travels through branching airways to reach tiny air sacs inside the lungs.")

## Air sacs exchange gases {image-left 1-1 title-in-image}

Tiny blood vessels called capillaries wrap around the air sacs. The walls of both the air sacs and the capillaries are very thin. Oxygen from the air passes through these walls and into the blood.

At the same time, carbon dioxide moves from the blood into the air sacs. You remove that carbon dioxide when you breathe out. Air does not flow directly into the blood. Oxygen and carbon dioxide cross the thin walls.

![An air sac and a separate capillary share thin walls. Oxygen moves from the air sac into blood; carbon dioxide moves from blood into the air sac.](/images/lungs-heart-and-blood/gas-exchange.webp "Gases cross the thin walls. Air does not flow into the blood vessel.")

## The heart pumps; vessels carry {stack title-in-image}

The circulatory system is made of the heart, blood, and blood vessels. The heart is a strong muscle that pumps blood. Blood vessels are the tubes that carry it.

- Arteries carry blood away from the heart.
- Veins bring blood back toward the heart.
- Capillaries are the tiniest vessels. They reach almost every cell so oxygen and nutrients can leave the blood and waste can enter it.

Arteries and veins are named by the direction blood travels relative to the heart, not by how much oxygen the blood carries.

## Follow the blood's journey {image-right 1-1 title-in-image}

Blood picks up oxygen in the lungs and carries it to the heart. The heart then pumps this oxygen-rich blood out through arteries to the rest of the body. At the body's capillaries, oxygen leaves the blood and reaches the cells. Carbon dioxide made by the cells enters the blood.

Blood carrying less oxygen returns through veins to the heart. The heart sends it to the lungs, where carbon dioxide leaves the blood and fresh oxygen enters it. The blood then returns to the heart, ready for another trip around the body.

The route repeats: heart to lungs, lungs to heart, heart to body, and body to heart.

![Two linked loops show blood moving from heart to lungs to heart, and from heart to body cells to heart. Bright red marks more oxygen and dark red marks less oxygen.](/images/lungs-heart-and-blood/blood-journey.webp "Follow the arrowheads around both loops. The route is simplified; blood is always red.")

## Working together

- The lungs bring air close to the blood so oxygen and carbon dioxide can be exchanged.
- The heart pumps blood to the lungs and the rest of the body.
- Blood vessels carry blood, and capillaries let materials move between blood and cells.

Together these systems keep a continuous loop going: the lungs supply oxygen, the heart and vessels deliver it, and the lungs remove carbon dioxide. Without this teamwork, your cells would not get the oxygen they need to stay alive.
