## Start a response quickly
Imagine touching an unexpectedly hot pan. Sensors in the skin detect a harmful stimulus. Sensory nerve signals enter the spinal cord, where connected nerve cells can activate motor pathways to muscles. Your hand begins to withdraw.

This is a **reflex**: a relatively automatic response through a nerve pathway. The signal does not need to wait for a conscious decision before the spinal circuit starts the movement.


**Read the picture:** Find the spinal cord below the brain, then a branching nerve route. Imagine a response beginning through spinal connections while another route carries information upward.

## The brain still matters
Information also travels toward the brain, where you become aware of the pain and situation. You can then make further choices, such as putting down an object or moving away. The brain also influences many reflex circuits.

So “the brain is not involved at all” is misleading. The key distinction is that the initial response can begin before conscious processing is complete.

## Compare two actions
Withdrawing from a hot object and deliberately reaching for a cup both use nerves and muscles. They differ in how the action is initiated and controlled.

**Trace the process:** Sensor → sensory pathway → spinal connections → motor pathway → muscle. Then add the route that carries information toward the brain.
