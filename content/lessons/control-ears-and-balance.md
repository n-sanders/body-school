## A chain of vibrations
Sound is a pressure wave moving through material such as air. The outer ear guides sound into the ear canal. Sound makes the eardrum vibrate. Three tiny middle-ear bones transfer vibrations toward the fluid-filled inner ear.

Inside a coiled structure called the **cochlea**, movement affects sensory hair cells. These cells help turn the vibration pattern into nerve signals. The brain uses the signals to perceive sound. You do not need the individual bone names to explain this chain.

## A second inner-ear job
Nearby inner-ear structures detect head movement and position. Their fluid and sensory cells respond when the head moves. This information helps with balance and keeping a steady view.

Balance also uses vision and information from muscles and joints. The ears do not act alone. A person may need to make more adjustments when one source of information is unavailable or misleading.

## Compare the jobs
Hearing begins with sound vibrations; balance uses information about movement and position. Both depend on sensory cells converting a physical change into signals.
