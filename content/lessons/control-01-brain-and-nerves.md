## Connected routes for information
The **brain** and **spinal cord** form the central part of the nervous system. Nerves connect them with the rest of the body. Sensory pathways bring information inward; motor pathways carry commands toward muscles and other responding tissues.

Nerve cells pass signals along their extensions and across small junctions between cells. A nerve is a bundle of many fibers, not one giant hollow pipe.

## Different regions cooperate
The large folded part of the brain supports thinking, memory, interpreting sensations, and voluntary actions. The smaller region toward the lower back helps coordinate movement and balance. The brainstem connects with the spinal cord and helps control essential automatic functions, including breathing.

These regions work together; a colored brain picture marks useful regions, not sealed compartments with only one task each.

## Follow a response
When you decide to pick up a cup, motor pathways help activate muscles. Sensory information about touch and position helps adjust your grip as the action continues.
