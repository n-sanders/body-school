Your body constantly receives information from the world and from inside itself, then decides how to respond. The nervous system carries that information and creates the responses. Its main parts are the brain and the nerves.

The brain is the control center. It sits inside your skull and is made of billions of nerve cells. Different areas of the brain handle different jobs. Some areas receive signals from your eyes, ears, skin, and other senses. Other areas decide what to do and send commands to your muscles. The brain also stores memories and lets you think, feel, and learn.

> **Activity idea:** Look at the brain diagram. Click on the areas that receive information from the eyes and the areas that send commands to the muscles.

Nerves are bundles of long, thin cells that act like wires. Sensory nerves carry messages from your body to the brain. They tell the brain about touch, temperature, pain, light, and sound. Motor nerves carry messages from the brain to your muscles and organs. They tell muscles when to contract so you can move or speak. Some nerves connect directly to the spinal cord, which is a thick bundle of nerves running down your back. The spinal cord can handle simple, fast responses called reflexes without waiting for the brain.

> **Activity idea:** Sort the pictures into two groups—sensory messages traveling to the brain and motor messages traveling away from the brain. Then match each picture to the correct nerve path.

Here is how a simple message travels:

- A sense organ detects something (for example, your hand touches something hot).
- A sensory nerve carries the signal to the spinal cord or brain.
- The brain or spinal cord decides on a response.
- A motor nerve carries the command to a muscle.
- The muscle moves (your hand pulls away).

The nervous system works at high speed so you can react quickly. It also lets you plan, remember, and control many actions on purpose. Without the brain and nerves, your body would not know what is happening around it and could not choose how to respond.
