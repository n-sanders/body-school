## Messages help your body respond

Your body constantly receives information from the world and from inside itself. The nervous system carries that information and helps your body respond. Its main parts are the brain, spinal cord, and nerves.

Some responses happen automatically. Others are actions you choose, such as catching a ball or saying hello.

## The brain is the control center {image-left 1-1 title-in-image}

The brain sits inside your skull and is made of billions of nerve cells. Different areas work together to do different jobs. Some receive information from your eyes, ears, skin, and other senses. Others help plan actions and send commands to muscles.

The brain also lets you think, feel, remember, and learn. You use it to recognize a friend's voice, remember a story, or decide where to throw a ball.

![A brain is surrounded by examples of receiving information from senses, directing movement, and remembering or learning.](/images/brain-and-nerves/brain-control.webp "Brain areas work together to receive information, direct movement, and help you remember and learn.")

## Messages travel in two directions {image-right 1-1 title-in-image}

Nerves are bundles of long, thin nerve fibers that act like wires carrying messages. Sensory nerves carry information from the body toward the brain or spinal cord. They carry messages about touch, temperature, pain, light, and sound.

Motor nerves carry commands from the brain or spinal cord toward muscles and organs. They tell muscles when to contract so you can move or speak.

The spinal cord is a thick bundle of nerve fibers that runs down your back inside the spine. It carries messages between the brain and much of the body. Some nerves, including those carrying information from the eyes, connect directly to the brain.

![A teal sensory route travels from the hand toward the spinal cord and brain. A separate orange motor route travels from the brain through the spinal cord toward a leg muscle.](/images/brain-and-nerves/message-directions.webp "Sensory messages travel inward. Motor commands travel outward toward muscles.")

## Reflexes help you respond quickly {image-left 1-1 title-in-image}

A reflex is a fast, automatic response. If your hand touches something hot, receptors in your skin detect it. A sensory nerve carries a message to your spinal cord.

The spinal cord can quickly send a command through a motor nerve to the muscles that pull your hand away. This response starts without waiting for your brain to choose what to do. Information also travels up to your brain, so you become aware of what happened.

![A hand pulls away from a hot cup. A teal sensory message reaches the spinal cord, and an orange motor command returns to an arm muscle. A separate teal branch carries information up to the brain.](/images/brain-and-nerves/spinal-reflex.webp "The spinal cord can start this reflex without waiting for a decision from the brain. The brain receives information too.")

## Actions you choose {stack title-in-image}

You also use the nervous system to choose actions. Imagine seeing a ball coming toward you. Your eyes detect it, and sensory nerves carry that information to your brain.

Your brain works out where the ball is going and chooses a response. It sends commands through the spinal cord and motor nerves to your arm and hand muscles. Those muscles contract so you can reach out and catch the ball.

The pattern is information coming in, a response being chosen, and a command going out to muscles.

![An eye notices a ball, a sensory message travels toward the brain, and a motor command travels toward arm muscles that help catch the ball.](/images/brain-and-nerves/chosen-action.webp "An action you choose: information comes in, the brain chooses a response, and a command goes out. This pathway is simplified.")

## Working together

- Sense receptors detect information from the world and inside the body.
- Sensory nerves carry that information toward the brain or spinal cord.
- The brain or spinal cord helps produce a response.
- Motor nerves carry commands toward muscles and organs.
- Muscles contract to create movement.

Some fast reflexes begin in the spinal cord. Actions you choose involve the brain planning a response. Your brain also helps you think, feel, remember, and learn.

The brain, spinal cord, and nerves work together so you can notice what is happening and respond.
