The reproductive system is made of the reproductive organs. Its job is to make it possible for a new baby to begin and grow. Boys and girls have different reproductive organs and different reproductive cells. Boys produce sperm cells and girls produce egg cells. Both an egg and a sperm are needed for a baby to start.

In girls the main organs are the ovaries, uterus, and vagina. The ovaries hold and release tiny egg cells. About once a month one egg leaves an ovary and travels into a tube that leads to the uterus. The uterus is a hollow, muscular organ. If an egg is joined by a sperm cell, it can attach to the wall of the uterus and begin to grow into a baby. The uterus stretches as the baby gets larger. The vagina is the passage that leads from the uterus to the outside of the body. When the baby is ready to be born, it travels through the vagina.

> **Activity idea:** Click on the ovaries and uterus in the diagram. Then match each organ to its job: stores and releases eggs, or provides a place for a baby to grow.

In boys the main organs are the testes and penis. The testes make sperm cells. Sperm are the cells that can join with an egg. The penis is the organ that can place sperm inside a girl's body so the sperm can reach an egg.

> **Activity idea:** Sort the pictures into two groups—girl reproductive organs and boy reproductive organs. Then match the testes to the job "makes sperm."

Here is the basic sequence:

- An ovary releases an egg.
- A sperm cell joins the egg.
- The joined cell attaches to the uterus.
- The uterus protects and feeds the growing baby.
- The baby is born through the vagina.

The reproductive organs do not work the same way every day the way the heart or lungs do. They become ready to do their job as a person grows older. Their special purpose is to start a new life. Without the reproductive organs, new babies could not begin.
