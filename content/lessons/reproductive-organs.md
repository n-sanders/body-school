## How new life begins

The reproductive system is made of the reproductive organs. Its job is to make it possible for a new baby to begin and grow. Two kinds of reproductive cells are needed: an egg cell and a sperm cell.

Female reproductive organs include the ovaries and uterus. Male reproductive organs include the testes and penis. Each organ has a particular job. Let's look at how they work together.

## Ovaries, tubes, and uterus {image-left 1-1 title-in-image}

The **ovaries** hold tiny egg cells. After puberty, an ovary usually releases an egg during each menstrual cycle. The egg enters a **fallopian tube**, a tube that leads toward the uterus.

The **uterus** is a hollow, muscular organ where a baby can grow. Its lower, narrow part is the **cervix**. The cervix opens into the **vagina**, a passage leading to the outside of the body.

The vagina is different from the urethra you learned about in the urinary lesson. Urine leaves through the urethra, not through the vagina.

![A front-view diagram shows two ovaries beside the fallopian tubes, the uterus in the center, the cervix below it, and the vagina leading downward.](/images/reproductive-organs/ovaries-and-uterus.webp "The ovaries release egg cells. The tubes lead toward the uterus. The cervix connects the uterus with the vagina. This diagram is simplified.")

## Testes and sperm {image-right 1-1 title-in-image}

The **testes**, also called testicles, make sperm cells after puberty. They sit inside a pouch of skin called the **scrotum**. Sperm travel through small tubes and mix with fluids to form **semen**.

The **penis** contains the urethra. This tube carries urine out of the body and also carries semen out at a different time. During sexual intercourse, semen can pass from the penis into the vagina. Sperm can then travel toward an egg.

The testes make sperm; the penis does not. The faint bladder in the diagram is part of the urinary system and is shown only to help you understand where the tubes are.

![A simplified side-view diagram shows a testis in the scrotum, a sperm-carrying tube, and the penis with the urethra running through it. A faint bladder gives urinary-system context.](/images/reproductive-organs/testes-and-sperm.webp "The testes make sperm. Tubes carry sperm, and semen can leave through the urethra in the penis. This is a simplified side view.")

## An egg and a sperm {stack title-in-image}

Egg and sperm cells are tiny. An egg cell is larger than a sperm cell. A sperm has a tail that helps it move.

When a sperm cell joins an egg cell, it is called **fertilization**. This usually happens in a fallopian tube. The fertilized egg then begins dividing into more cells as it travels toward the uterus.

![An enlarged egg cell and a sperm cell are labeled beside a separate sequence showing a fertilized egg beginning to divide into two cells.](/images/reproductive-organs/egg-and-sperm.webp "Egg and sperm cells are greatly enlarged and are not shown to scale. After fertilization, the fertilized egg begins dividing into more cells.")

## A place to grow

Here is the basic sequence:

1. An ovary releases an egg, which enters a fallopian tube.
2. If a sperm joins the egg, fertilization occurs.
3. The fertilized egg divides into more cells as it travels toward the uterus.
4. It can attach to the lining of the uterus. This is called **implantation**. It continues developing into an embryo and later a fetus, the names for a developing baby at different stages.
5. The baby grows in the uterus and receives oxygen and nutrients through the **placenta** and **umbilical cord**. The uterus stretches as the baby grows.

Many babies are born through the vagina after the cervix opens. Some babies are born through an operation called a cesarean birth, or C-section.

## Growing and changing

**Puberty** is the time when the body changes and reproductive organs begin to mature. Ovaries begin releasing eggs, and testes begin making sperm. These changes happen at different ages for different people.

The reproductive organs are parts of the body, just like the other organs in these lessons. Learning their names helps you understand the body and ask clear questions.

## Working together

Ovaries hold and release egg cells. Testes make sperm cells. A sperm and an egg can join during fertilization, and a developing baby can grow in the uterus after implantation. Each organ has its own job in making reproduction possible.
