## Turning food into fuel

Your body needs fuel to move, grow, and stay warm. The digestive system breaks down the food you eat into nutrients your body can absorb and use. It also removes the solid waste that is left over. The stomach, intestines, and liver all help with these jobs.

## From your mouth to your stomach {image-right 1-1 title-in-image}

Food enters through your mouth, where teeth and saliva start to break it down. It then travels down a tube called the esophagus and into the stomach.

The stomach is a muscular sac that churns the food and mixes it with strong digestive juices. This mixing and the juices turn the food into a thick liquid, ready to move into the small intestine.

![Orange arrows trace food from the mouth down the esophagus into the stomach, where curved arrows show churning and mixing with digestive juices.](/images/stomach-intestines-and-liver/mouth-to-stomach.webp "Food travels down the esophagus. The stomach churns it and mixes it with digestive juices.")

## The small intestine absorbs nutrients {image-left 1-1 title-in-image}

The liquid food next moves into the small intestine. This is a long, coiled tube where most of the nutrients are absorbed into the blood.

The walls of the small intestine are covered with tiny finger-like projections that help grab the nutrients. These projections are called villi. Nutrients cross the intestinal wall and enter nearby blood vessels, which carry them to other parts of the body.

![A coiled small intestine is enlarged to show finger-like villi. Nutrient particles cross a villus surface and enter a nearby blood vessel.](/images/stomach-intestines-and-liver/small-intestine.webp "Tiny finger-like villi help grab nutrients from liquid food so the body can absorb them.")

## The large intestine absorbs water {image-right 1-1 title-in-image}

Material that has not been absorbed moves into the large intestine. The large intestine absorbs water and packs the remaining material into solid waste.

That waste is stored in the rectum until it leaves the body through the anus. The large intestine is wider than the small intestine, even though it is shorter.

![Orange arrows follow the large intestine toward the rectum and anus. An inset shows water crossing the intestinal wall into a blood vessel.](/images/stomach-intestines-and-liver/large-intestine.webp "The large intestine absorbs water and moves remaining waste toward the rectum and anus.")

## The liver helps from the sidelines {stack title-in-image}

The liver is a large organ that helps digestion even though food does not pass through it. It makes a liquid called bile. Bile can be stored in the gallbladder and released into the small intestine.

Bile breaks large fat droplets into smaller droplets. This helps digestive enzymes break down the fats so they can be absorbed. Enzymes are substances that help break food into smaller parts.

The liver also processes many of the nutrients that come from the small intestine and stores some of them for later use.

![Orange arrows carry food through the stomach into the small intestine. A separate green bile duct carries bile from the liver and gallbladder into the small intestine after the stomach.](/images/stomach-intestines-and-liver/liver-and-bile.webp "Food and bile follow different routes. Food does not pass through the liver or gallbladder. This diagram is simplified and not to scale.")

## Working together

Here is the food pathway in order:

- Mouth: teeth and saliva start breaking food down.
- Esophagus: carries food to the stomach.
- Stomach: churns food and mixes it with digestive juices.
- Small intestine: absorbs most nutrients.
- Large intestine: absorbs water and forms solid waste.
- Rectum and anus: store and release the waste.

The liver helps alongside this pathway by making bile and processing nutrients. Food does not pass through the liver.

Without the digestive system, the food you eat would stay too large for your cells to use. The stomach, intestines, and liver work together so your body gets the energy it needs and gets rid of what it does not.
