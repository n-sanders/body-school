Your body needs fuel to move, grow, and stay warm. The digestive system breaks down the food you eat so your body can absorb that fuel and remove the solid waste that is left over. The main organs are the stomach, intestines, and liver.

Food enters through your mouth, where teeth and saliva start to break it into smaller pieces. It then travels down a tube called the esophagus and into the stomach. The stomach is a muscular sac that churns the food and mixes it with strong juices. These juices turn the food into a thick liquid.

> **Activity idea:** Put the pictures of the digestive organs in the correct order from mouth to the end of the system.

The liquid food next moves into the small intestine. This is a long, coiled tube where most of the nutrients are absorbed into the blood. The walls of the small intestine are covered with tiny finger-like projections that help grab the nutrients. Anything that cannot be absorbed continues into the large intestine. The large intestine absorbs water and packs the remaining material into solid waste that leaves the body.

The liver is a large organ that helps digestion even though food does not pass through it. It produces a liquid called bile that is stored in the gallbladder and released into the small intestine. Bile helps break down fats so they can be absorbed. The liver also processes many of the nutrients that come from the small intestine and stores some of them for later use.

> **Activity idea:** Match each organ picture to its main job: churn food, absorb nutrients, absorb water, or help break down fats.

Here is the path in simple steps:

- Mouth starts breaking food down.
- Stomach mixes and liquefies it.
- Small intestine absorbs the fuel.
- Large intestine removes solid waste.
- Liver assists by making bile and processing nutrients.

Without the digestive system, the food you eat would stay too large for your cells to use. The stomach, intestines, and liver work together so your body gets the energy it needs and gets rid of what it does not.
