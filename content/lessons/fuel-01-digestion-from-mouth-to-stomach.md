## Start with the sandwich

Do not begin with a list of organs. Begin with lunch. You bite a sandwich. Two kinds of breakdown start at once.

**Mechanical digestion** is physical. Teeth crush and tear. The tongue mixes. The pieces get smaller, which gives chemicals more surface to work on.

**Chemical digestion** uses enzymes and, later, acid. Saliva is not just water. It has enzymes that start breaking starch. The food is still far from being fuel a cell can use.

## The trip to the stomach

Swallowing sends the bite into the **esophagus**. Smooth muscle there squeezes in waves. That wave is **peristalsis**. Food movement does not rely only on gravity; coordinated muscle waves move it along.

The **stomach** is a J-shaped muscular sac. It does three things:

- It stores the meal so you do not have to absorb a whole sandwich at once.
- It churns the food. That is more mechanical digestion.
- It mixes in acid and enzymes, including pepsin, which starts protein breakdown. That is chemical digestion.

A mucus lining keeps the acid from digesting the stomach wall itself. The stomach gradually releases a soupy mixture into the small intestine; emptying time varies with the meal.

The blood will eventually carry the nutrients from this sandwich. Digestive substances from the liver and pancreas help prepare nutrients for absorption across the small-intestinal lining.
