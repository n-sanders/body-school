## Help from outside the tube
Food goes from the stomach to the small intestine. It does not travel through the liver, gallbladder, or pancreas. These organs help by sending substances through small tubes called ducts.

The **liver** makes bile. The **gallbladder** stores and concentrates bile and releases it into the small intestine. Bile helps separate large fat droplets into smaller droplets, giving digestive enzymes more surface to work on. Bile is not itself a fat-digesting enzyme.

The **pancreas** sends digestive enzymes into the small intestine. Enzymes help break large food molecules into smaller ones. Pancreatic fluid also helps neutralize the acidic mixture arriving from the stomach, creating better conditions for intestinal digestion.

## One organ, several roles
The liver also processes absorbed materials and performs many other jobs. The pancreas has a second role: releasing hormones into blood. A duct carrying digestive fluid and blood carrying a hormone are different routes.
