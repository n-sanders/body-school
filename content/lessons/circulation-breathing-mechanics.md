## Moving air is a mechanical job
Your lungs do not pull air in by squeezing inward. During a quiet breath in, the **diaphragm**, a broad muscle below the lungs, contracts and moves downward. Muscles between the ribs also help expand the chest.

As chest space increases, the lungs expand with it. Air pressure inside the lungs becomes slightly lower than outside pressure, so air flows inward through the airways.

## Breathing out
During a quiet breath out, the breathing muscles relax. The lungs and chest tend to spring back, reducing space. Pressure inside rises slightly above outside pressure, and air flows out. Strong breathing out can also use muscles actively.

Breathing faster means more breaths per minute. Breathing more deeply means moving more air with each breath. These can change separately. Neither description alone tells you everything about gas exchange.

## Use a model carefully
A stretchy bag inside a sealed container can model how changing surrounding space affects a lung. Real lungs have branching airways, many tiny air sacs, a blood supply, and connections to living chest tissues that a simple bag leaves out.

**Explain aloud:** Trace the chain from diaphragm contraction to inward airflow.
