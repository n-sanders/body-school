## Follow light, then follow a signal
Light enters through the clear front of the eye and passes through the **pupil**, an opening. The colored ring around it adjusts the opening’s size. The clear front surface and the **lens** help focus light onto the **retina**, the light-sensitive tissue at the back.

Cells in the retina respond to light. Connected cells produce nerve signals that travel toward the brain through the optic nerve. The brain uses this information to form visual perception.

The picture is therefore not simply a tiny scene that travels down a hollow nerve. Light is changed into information carried by cells.

## Two different adjustments
Changing pupil size changes how much light enters. Changing lens shape helps focus on objects at different distances. Letting in more light and sharpening focus are related to seeing, but they are different jobs.

## Connect sensation with action
To catch a ball, vision supplies information about position and motion. The brain combines it with other sensory information and sends commands toward muscles.

**Explain aloud:** Why could a clear lens alone not provide vision if the light-sensitive tissue could not send usable signals?
