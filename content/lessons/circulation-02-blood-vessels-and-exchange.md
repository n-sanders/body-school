## Three kinds of tube

The heart is the pump. **Blood vessels** are the roads. Learning three names covers the pattern:

### Read the picture

Follow the red route into the fine blood-vessel network and out through the blue route. The separate green branches collect tissue fluid into lymph vessels.

![Small exchange vessels connect incoming and outgoing blood routes. Green represents lymph vessels; it is a separate route rather than another kind of blood vessel.](images/servier/vessels.png)

- **Arteries** carry blood **away** from the heart. Their walls are strong because the push is strong.
- **Capillaries** are tiny and thin. This is where oxygen, nutrients, and wastes cross between blood and cells.
- **Veins** carry blood **back** toward the heart. Many of them have **valves** so the blood does not slide backward.

You do not need the name of every artery. You need the direction and the job.

## A drop's errand

Leave the left ventricle in an artery. Branch after branch, the tube gets smaller until you are in a capillary beside a working muscle cell. Oxygen and nutrients slip out. Carbon dioxide and other wastes slip in. Then the capillary joins small veins, the veins join larger ones, and the blood returns to the right side of the heart.

That same blood will be sent to the lungs next, because the oxygen it just gave away has to be replaced. The lungs and the diaphragm are the rest of this unit.

The nutrients in the drop are a preview of Unit 4. Digestion breaks food down, and absorption transfers nutrients across the intestinal lining into transport routes. Circulation only delivers them.


**Reflect:** Follow blood from an artery through capillaries and back toward the heart. Where do materials cross, and why there?
