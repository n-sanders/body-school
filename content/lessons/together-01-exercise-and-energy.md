## One sprint, many systems

You do not grow a new organ for soccer or a flight of stairs. The systems you already studied just change their pace together.

![What each system contributes during exercise](images/lessons/together-01-exercise-chain.svg)

*Demand, delivery, coordination, and cooling. (Diagram made for Body School.)*

Walk through a sprint:

- **Skeletal muscles** pull on bones. They only pull, so pairs of muscles keep the joints moving. They demand more oxygen and fuel.
- **Lungs and diaphragm** move more air. At the alveoli, oxygen enters the blood and carbon dioxide leaves. That is the same exchange as Unit 3, done faster.
- The **heart** beats faster and harder. **Arteries** carry the oxygen-rich blood out. **Capillaries** in the muscles are where the exchange with cells happens.
- The **nervous system** coordinates the decision to run and many of the automatic adjustments. **Adrenal glands** can add adrenaline, a slower broadcast that supports the effort.
- **Skin** sweats, and blood flow in the skin helps dump heat so temperature stays in range. That is homeostasis from Unit 5, using the integumentary system from Unit 6.

Fuel for the sprint was stored from earlier meals. If the run is long, the body starts asking for more of that stored fuel. The next lesson follows a meal and the cleanup after it, including a small injury healing while you rest.
