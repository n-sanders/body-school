Your body has a strong framework made of bones, joints, and muscles. Together they form the skeletal and muscular systems. Their main jobs are to protect important organs, support your body so you can stand up, and move you around.

Bones are hard and sturdy. They make up the skeleton, which is the framework inside you. Your skull protects your brain. Your ribs protect your heart and lungs. Your backbone, called the spine, holds you upright and protects the spinal cord. Bones also store minerals that your body needs and help make new blood cells inside the marrow.

> **Activity idea:** Look at the skeleton picture. Click on the bones that protect the brain, heart, and lungs.

Joints are the places where two bones meet. Some joints, like your elbows and knees, bend and straighten so you can move. Other joints, like the ones in your skull, stay fixed and do not move. Cartilage, a smooth tissue, covers the ends of bones at movable joints so they can glide without grinding.

Muscles are soft tissues that pull on bones to create movement. You have voluntary muscles that you control, such as the ones in your arms and legs, and involuntary muscles that work without you thinking, such as the heart muscle. When a muscle contracts, it shortens and pulls a bone. When it relaxes, the bone can move back. Most movements need pairs of muscles working together—one pulls one way, the other pulls the opposite way.

> **Activity idea:** Match each muscle picture to the joint or bone it moves. Then group the pictures into "protects," "supports," or "moves."

Here is how the three parts work as a team:

- Bones give shape and protection.
- Joints let the bones move or stay connected.
- Muscles supply the force that moves the bones.

Without bones you would have no structure. Without joints you could not bend. Without muscles you could not walk, jump, or even smile. The skeletal and muscular systems keep your body strong, safe, and able to move every day.
