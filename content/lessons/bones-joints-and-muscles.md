## Your body's framework {image-right 1-1 title-in-image}

Your body has a strong framework made of bones, joints, and muscles. Together they form the skeletal and muscular systems. Their main jobs are to protect important organs, support your body so you can stand up, and move you around.

![A skeleton and a view of skeletal muscles stand side by side. Bones support and protect the body; skeletal muscles move it.](/images/bones-joints-and-muscles/framework.webp "Bones support and protect your body. Skeletal muscles help you move.")

## Bones support and protect you {image-left 2-1 title-in-image}

Bones are hard and sturdy. They make up the skeleton, which is the framework inside you. Your skull protects your brain. Your ribs protect your heart and lungs. Your backbone, called the spine, holds you upright and protects the spinal cord.

Bones also store minerals that your body needs. Inside many bones, a soft tissue called marrow helps make new blood cells.

![Three panels show a skull cutaway surrounding the brain, an intact rib cage in front of the heart and lungs, and vertebrae protecting the spinal cord.](/images/bones-joints-and-muscles/bones-protect.webp "Bones protect important organs inside your body.")

## Joints connect bones {image-right 1-2 title-in-image}

Joints are the places where two bones meet. Some joints, like your elbows and knees, bend and straighten so you can move. Other joints, like the ones between the bones of your skull, stay fixed and do not move.

Cartilage, a smooth tissue, covers the ends of bones at movable joints so they can glide without grinding.

![A movable knee joint shows the thigh bone, shin bone, and smooth cartilage. An inset shows a fixed joint between skull bones.](/images/bones-joints-and-muscles/joints.webp "Some joints allow movement. Others hold bones firmly together.")

## Muscles pull to make movement {stack title-in-image}

Skeletal muscles are soft tissues that pull on bones to create movement. When a muscle contracts, it shortens and pulls a bone. Muscles pull; they cannot push bones back.

Most movements need pairs of muscles working together. To bend your elbow, a muscle at the front of your upper arm contracts while the muscle at the back relaxes. To straighten your elbow, the muscle at the back contracts while the muscle at the front relaxes. One pulls one way, and the other pulls the opposite way.

![Two views of an arm show opposing muscles. When bending the elbow, the front muscle contracts and the back muscle relaxes. When straightening, their roles reverse.](/images/bones-joints-and-muscles/muscle-pairs.webp "Muscles pull on bones. Opposing muscles move the arm in different directions.")

The skeletal muscles in your arms and legs are voluntary muscles: you can choose to move them. You also have involuntary muscles that work without you thinking about them. Your heart muscle, for example, contracts to pump blood.

## Working together

Here is how the three parts work as a team:

- Bones give shape and protection.
- Joints connect bones. Movable joints let bones move, while fixed joints hold them firmly together.
- Skeletal muscles supply the force that moves the bones.

Without bones you would have no structure. Without movable joints you could not bend your arms or legs. Without muscles you could not walk, jump, or even smile. The skeletal and muscular systems keep your body strong, safe, and able to move every day.
