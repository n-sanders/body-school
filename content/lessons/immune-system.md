Your body has a defense system that finds and fights germs that can make you sick. This is the immune system. It uses special white blood cells and several organs that help those cells do their work. Its main job is to protect you from illness.

White blood cells are the main fighters. Some white blood cells patrol the body looking for germs. When they find one, they mark it so other cells know it is an invader. Other white blood cells then attack and destroy the germ. Some white blood cells also remember germs they have already fought, so the next time the same germ appears they can respond faster.

> **Activity idea:** Sort the cell pictures into two groups—white blood cells that find germs and white blood cells that destroy germs.

Several organs support these cells. Bone marrow, found inside certain bones, makes new white blood cells. The thymus, a small organ in the chest, helps some white blood cells mature and learn which cells belong to your body and which do not. Lymph nodes are small, bean-shaped structures located in places such as your neck, armpits, and groin. They filter fluid and give white blood cells a place to gather and fight germs. The spleen, an organ in the upper left side of your abdomen, also filters blood and stores white blood cells ready for action.

> **Activity idea:** Click on the bone marrow, thymus, lymph nodes, and spleen in the body diagram. Then match each organ to its main job: makes white blood cells, trains white blood cells, or filters and gathers fighters.

Here is how the system responds to a germ:

- A white blood cell finds the germ.
- It signals other white blood cells.
- More white blood cells arrive and destroy the germ.
- Some cells remember the germ for next time.

The immune system works quietly most of the time. When it is fighting hard you may notice swollen lymph nodes or a fever. Without these special cells and organs, germs would be much harder for your body to stop.
