## Your body's defense system

Your body has a defense system that finds and fights germs that can make you sick. This is the **immune system**. It uses special white blood cells and several organs that help those cells do their work.

The skin helps keep germs out in the first place. If germs get past that barrier, defenses inside your body can respond. These defenses work together to help protect you from illness.

## White blood cells work together {image-left 1-1 title-in-image}

White blood cells have different, overlapping jobs. Some can surround and swallow germs. This is called **engulfing**. Some release signals that help other immune cells respond.

Certain white blood cells make **antibodies**. These are proteins that attach to particular targets on germs. Antibodies can block germs or help other parts of the immune system remove them.

A cell can do more than one job. The pictures show examples of teamwork, not separate groups of cells that only find or only destroy germs.

## Organs that support the defense {image-right 1-1 title-in-image}

**Bone marrow**, soft tissue inside certain bones, makes new white blood cells. It also makes other kinds of blood cells.

The **thymus** is a small organ in the upper chest. It helps **T cells**, a kind of white blood cell, mature and learn to avoid attacking your body's own cells.

**Lymph nodes** are small, bean-shaped structures found in places such as your neck, armpits, and groin. They filter **lymph**, fluid collected from around body tissues. They also provide places where immune cells can meet and respond to germs.

The **spleen** sits in the upper left side of your abdomen, under the ribs. It filters blood and helps immune cells respond to germs carried in the blood. Lymph nodes filter lymph; the spleen filters blood.

## Responding to a germ {stack title-in-image}

Here is one simplified example of how the immune system responds:

1. An immune cell recognizes a germ.
2. It sends signals that help call other immune cells.
3. More cells respond and work together.
4. The cells and other defenses help remove the germ.

Different germs can trigger different responses. Many parts of the immune system can work at the same time.

![Four panels show a cell recognizing a germ, releasing signals, more immune cells responding, and a cell engulfing the germ.](/images/immune-system/germ-response.webp "Recognize, signal, respond, and remove. This is one simplified example, not a rule that every immune response follows exactly.")

## Remembering for next time {image-left 1-1 title-in-image}

After an infection, some white blood cells remain as **memory cells**. If the same germ returns, these cells can help the body respond faster.

Immune memory does not mean you can never get sick from that germ again. It gives the body a head start, rather than a guarantee of protection.

## Working together

White blood cells and immune organs work as a team. Bone marrow makes cells, the thymus helps T cells mature, and lymph nodes and the spleen provide places for immune responses.

The immune system works quietly much of the time. During an infection, you may notice swollen lymph nodes or a fever. These can be signs of the body's response. Without these defenses, germs would be much harder for your body to stop.
