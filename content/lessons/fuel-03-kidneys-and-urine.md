## Not a waste dump

Follow a glass of water. It is absorbed into the blood. Cells are bathed in that blood's water. If you drank more than today's cells need, something has to decide what stays.

The **kidneys** are a pair of bean-shaped filters at the back of the abdomen. They filter blood all day. Then they **reabsorb** the water, salts, and nutrients the body should keep. What remains — wastes such as urea, plus extra water and salts — becomes **urine**.

That is a careful sort, not a hole in the bottom of the bloodstream.

![Kidneys beside keep and release lists](images/lessons/fuel-03-kidney-filter.svg)

*Keep what the blood still needs. Release wastes and extra water. (Diagram made for Body School.)*

## The path out

1. Each kidney drains through a **ureter**.
2. Urine is stored in the **bladder**.
3. It leaves through the **urethra**.

In adults the urinary and reproductive systems share some pathways. This course does **not** study reproductive anatomy. The urethra is mentioned only as the way urine leaves.

![A nephron, the kidney's tiny filter](images/lessons/sourced-nephron.png)

The kidneys also help with blood pressure and the balance of salts. Nerves and hormones decide when these adjustments happen. That control system is the next unit.
