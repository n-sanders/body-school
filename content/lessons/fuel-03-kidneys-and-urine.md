## Filtering is only the beginning
Blood reaches the kidneys through blood vessels. Inside are many tiny processing units. Small substances and water can pass from blood into a filtering space, while blood cells and most large proteins normally remain in the bloodstream.

That first filtered fluid contains useful materials as well as wastes. The kidney returns much of the water and many needed substances to blood. It also transfers some additional substances from blood into the processing tubes.

The fluid left after these adjustments becomes **urine**. Filtration alone would lose far too much useful material; selective return is essential.

## Follow the right route
Urine leaves each kidney through a **ureter**, a tube leading to the bladder. The **bladder** stores urine. A separate tube, the **urethra**, carries urine out of the body. The two tube names look similar, but follow the route rather than memorize their spelling.

Kidneys process blood; the bladder is a storage organ. Neither receives whole undigested food from the intestine.
