Unit 2: Support and Movement — Skeletal and Muscular Systems
Approach

Show how the skeleton provides structure, protection, and attachment points while muscles produce movement by pulling on bones. Introduce major bones and joints in a practical way, then connect them to the three kinds of muscle tissue. Emphasize that bones and muscles work as a team; neither system can move the body alone.

Key concepts

The skeletal system is made of bones, cartilage, and ligaments. Its main jobs are:
- Support the body and give it shape.
- Protect soft organs (skull protects the brain, rib cage protects the heart and lungs).
- Provide places for muscles to attach.
- Store minerals (especially calcium) and produce blood cells in bone marrow.

Bones meet at joints. Some joints allow little or no movement (skull sutures); others allow a wide range (shoulder, hip, knee). Cartilage cushions many joints.

The muscular system includes three types of muscle:
- Skeletal muscle attaches to bones and moves the body under voluntary control.
- Cardiac muscle forms the heart and contracts automatically.
- Smooth muscle lines organs such as the stomach and blood vessels and also works automatically.

Skeletal muscles work in pairs (one contracts while the other relaxes) and pull, never push. Tendons connect muscle to bone. The skeleton provides the rigid framework that muscles pull against to create movement.

Tone guidance for lessons

Start with everyday movement (standing up, throwing a ball, breathing) and ask which body parts are involved before naming systems.
Use simple comparisons (a lever and a pulley, or a tent pole and the ropes that pull it) to illustrate how bones and muscles interact.
Name a handful of major bones and muscle groups rather than trying to catalog every one.
Highlight protection and blood-cell production as jobs of the skeleton that are easy to overlook.
End by noting that movement requires energy and oxygen, which the next units will address through circulation and breathing.
