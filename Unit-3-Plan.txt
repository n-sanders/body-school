Unit 3: Circulation and Breathing — Circulatory and Respiratory Systems
Approach

Treat the heart, blood vessels, and lungs as a coordinated delivery and exchange system. First show how the heart pumps blood through vessels to deliver oxygen and nutrients and remove wastes. Then show how the lungs bring in oxygen and release carbon dioxide. Emphasize that the two systems meet at the tiny air sacs in the lungs and must work together continuously.

Key concepts

The circulatory (cardiovascular) system consists of the heart, blood vessels, and blood.
- The heart is a muscular pump with four chambers: two atria (upper) and two ventricles (lower). It sends blood to the lungs and to the rest of the body.
- Arteries carry blood away from the heart; veins carry it back; capillaries are the tiny vessels where exchange happens.
- Blood carries oxygen, nutrients, hormones, and wastes. Red blood cells carry most of the oxygen; white blood cells help fight infection; platelets help clotting.

The respiratory system brings air into the body and exchanges gases.
- Air travels through the nose or mouth, trachea (windpipe), bronchi, and into the lungs.
- In the lungs, tiny air sacs called alveoli are surrounded by capillaries. Oxygen moves into the blood; carbon dioxide moves out.
- Breathing is driven by the diaphragm and rib muscles. The rate changes with activity because cells need more oxygen when they work harder.

The two systems are tightly linked: blood that has picked up oxygen in the lungs is pumped by the heart to the rest of the body, and blood returning from the body is sent back to the lungs to unload carbon dioxide and reload oxygen.

Tone guidance for lessons

Begin with the feeling of a heartbeat or the need to breathe harder after running, then connect those sensations to the organs involved.
Use simple flow diagrams or “follow a drop of blood” / “follow a breath of air” sequences.
Name the major chambers of the heart and the path of air without requiring students to memorize every vessel name at this stage.
Stress that the systems run all the time, day and night, and adjust automatically to the body’s needs.
End by noting that the nutrients the blood also carries come from the digestive system, which is covered next.
